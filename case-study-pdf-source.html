The challenge

The advertiser ran its first in-arena campaign with the network. Ahead of renewal, both sides wanted a clear answer: did more people go from these arenas to the restaurants than the year before? Impressions and attendance could not answer that.

What we did

  • Used a natural baseline. The campaign ran in year two but not year one, so we compared January to April across both years.
  • Measured the exposed venues. Visits from the network's arenas to the chain's locations in both periods.
  • Built two comparison groups. Arenas outside the network, plus a random general population sample used to index visitation.
  • Reported trends, not headcounts. Indexed percentage change from privacy-compliant, aggregated location data.

The results

  • Visits from network arenas to the chain rose 12.1% year over year (Jan to Apr), with March up 13.6% and April up 39.6%.
  • Over the same period, visits from arenas outside the network fell 1.3%.
  • Year over year, the network's arenas grew visits 35.9% more than arenas outside the network, and 204% more than the general population benchmark.
  • Delivered as a concise, shareable summary in under two weeks.
  • The advertiser renewed for 2027.

Why it matters

Venue and out-of-home media have always struggled to connect exposure to outcomes. With a clean baseline and the right comparison groups, the network showed measurable growth in real visits, not just impressions, and turned a first-time advertiser into a returning one.
